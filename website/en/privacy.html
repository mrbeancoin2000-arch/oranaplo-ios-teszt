2. What data is involved?

Health data you explicitly authorize in Apple Health: potentially heart-rate samples, HRV, workout and sleep details, body measurements, recorded blood pressure, steps and fluid intake. Health profile access is requested separately. The app may also handle your Google account identifier, Google access tokens via its SDK, local sync state and Apple StoreKit entitlement.

3. Where does it go?

The app reads approved HealthKit samples on your device and sends selected exports directly to your own Google spreadsheet via Google APIs. The local queue may temporarily contain health measurements. There is no developer-operated central health-data server in the current design. The optional Apps Script runs within your Google spreadsheet.

4. External service providers

Apple provides App Store, StoreKit and HealthKit. Google provides Google Sign-In, Sheets and optional Drive APIs. Providers may process data under their own terms. Current code does not include an advertising SDK; re-audit every release.

5. Permissions and controls

You can grant or withdraw Health access by type in iOS. Disconnect Google in the app or revoke access from Google Account settings. Exporting the health profile to Sheets requires explicit confirmation. The lawful bases and special-category health-data conditions require independent review before release.

6. Retention and deletion

The device may retain a pending upload queue and deduplication IDs. Deleting the app normally removes its local container, but not your Google spreadsheet or Apple subscription. You can delete the spreadsheet from your Google account. Deleting a HealthKit sample does not always automatically remove a previously exported spreadsheet row in the current prototype.

7. Security and changes

iOS file protection and Google OAuth are used. Health data must not be used for advertising. Before public release, audit every SDK, retention, deletion workflow and GDPR requirements. Update this policy whenever actual behavior changes.
